# Corrections

Every claim this project has withdrawn, why, and what replaced it. Retractions
are marked in place in the original documents rather than edited away — the
record of what was believed and why it was wrong is part of the evidence.

| # | Claim | Withdrawn because | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generic error-bounded compressors underperform on LLM state because their inductive bias assumes one exploitable structure | SZ3 compresses the same tensors better than this project's codec. But the reason was misattributed twice: A3 attributed the advantage to quantisation and entropy coding rather than prediction, on a 14.4% margin that a full configuration scan on Gate B0 data cut to 2.9% and split by kind — see correction 18 | `superseded/d8_kv_findings.md`, retracted by D14 |
| 2 | The advantage is implementation, not algorithm — a simple codec that maps onto a GPU beats a better one that does not | cuSZp is a better algorithm *and* maps onto a GPU: 2–3× the ratio at ~9× the speed | `superseded/d14_table_b_findings.md`, retracted by D14b |
| 3 | Cross-tensor mean compression ratios at a fixed absolute ε | Per-tensor std spans 115× across this corpus, so a shared absolute ε is a different bound on every tensor; the means are not comparisons | `d13`, `d14`, `d14b`, `d20`, corrected in Phase A |
| 4 | Break-even at 19.6 GB/s | Stated unconditionally. It excludes bf16↔fp32 conversion and is unvalidated below 4 MB, the smallest payload the link was measured at | `superseded/d9_codec_findings.md`, conditioned in Phase A |
| 5 | Dtype conversion adds 37% | 37% is the surcharge on encode+decode; as a share of codec-side time it is 27%. Both come from memory-bandwidth arithmetic — **no GPU timing exists** | withdrawn in Phase A, measurement moved to Phase C |
| 6 | An online controller bypasses in 69% of cases | The controller reads measured compressed bytes, so it is an offline oracle; 69% is the share of equal-weighted sweep cells, not of requests | `superseded/d21_policy_findings.md`, relabelled in Phase A |
| 7 | SZ3's ratio (at the `pysz` default configuration) | The default under-reports SZ3 by up to 9.5% against a documented scan | `superseded/a3_sz3_config_findings.md` |
| 8 | cuSZp `fixed` mode has 3–6× cross-process time variance | Measured cold start, not the mode. All modes share a ~0.88 ms warm median; cold start is 347× that, and the tails belong to a shared virtualised GPU | `b0_corpus_findings.md`, withdrawn by B2 |
| 9 | Break-even 4.83 GB/s, and "measured" | Computed as `raw_bytes / total_time`, which is not a break-even. The quantity is `bytes_saved / codec_cost` → 3.42–4.25 GB/s depending on what counts as codec cost — and it is model-implied, not measured: only one link speed was exercised | `c_transport_findings.md` |
| 10 | "180× different quality cost" between K and V | The denominator's CI crosses zero, so the ratio is arbitrary. K-only degrades significantly; V-only shows no detectable degradation | `d_quality_findings.md` |
| 11 | "The one mechanism able to flip the decision is unavailable" | The gate measured same-GPU encode/encode concurrency, not the cross-GPU pipeline it stood in for | `superseded/e_pipeline_findings.md` |
| 12 | cuSZp violates its error bound on up to 32.5% of tensors | It does not. cuSZp requires zeroed buffers — it skips elements it expects to be zero and reads past `cmpSize` — and `torch.empty` put the allocator's leftovers in the reconstruction. Measured correctly: 0 violations, worst error exactly 1.000× ε | `remeasure_findings.md` |
| 13 | K and V interact super-additively; contrast +5.77 | Same cause. The collapse appeared at loose `c_V` because that is where the decoder leaves the most unwritten. Corrected contrasts +0.0012 and −0.0027, both crossing zero: **no interaction** | `superseded/asymmetric_kv_findings.md`, withdrawn in full |
| 14 | K-only +138% vs V-only −0.76% perplexity | Direction survives, magnitude does not: K-only costs +0.0228 NLL, ~0.15% of perplexity, and both kinds at c = 0.10 ship 0.33× the bytes with no detectable degradation | `d_quality_findings.md` |
| 15 | Closed E2E 46.84 ms, break-even 4.06 GB/s | The staging buffer overwrote itself, so 55 of 56 receivers got the wrong payload. Corrected: raw 23.64 ms, compressed 50.48 ms, break-even 3.82 GB/s | `session_close_findings.md` |
| 16 | Cross-GPU overlap efficiency 30%, encode 7.46 ms, decode 6.01 ms | Re-measured over 56 tensors with zeroed buffers and a correctness gate: **53.7%** two-threaded (IQR 46.6–62.1), 1.4% single-threaded. The two absolute times were totals of 20 iterations — per call they are 0.373 and 0.301 ms. The conclusion survives on a stronger footing: overlap can save at most 15.01 ms where 26.84 ms is needed, so **no efficiency reaches the raw baseline** | `session_close_findings.md`, superseded by `gate2_findings.md` |
| 17 | The B0 manifest's per-observation cuSZp error columns | Measured against `torch.empty` before the zeroed-buffer contract was known. 32.5% carry a false `within_eps_fp32`; 14.6% report an identical error across all nine (c, mode) cells, which no real measurement does. Re-measured properly: 0 violations. **`cmp_bytes` and every ratio derived from it are unaffected** | `b0_corpus_findings.md` |
| 18 | Turning SZ3's prediction off beats every predictor by 14.4%; SZ3's advantage on KV cache is in quantisation and entropy coding, not prediction | Two thirds of that margin was the corpus and one third was an unscanned configuration space. Against the whole grid `pysz` reaches, on the Gate B0 prefill capture, the margin is **+2.9%** frozen and **+1.9%** against a per-tensor oracle. And it is not a property of KV cache: no-prediction beats every predictor on **28/28 V tensors** and loses on **22/28 K tensors**, because the token axis carries adj/std 0.50 for K against 1.10 for V | `superseded/a3_sz3_config_findings.md`, superseded by `a3_sz3_full_findings.md` |
| 19 | "Nor did V-only *improve* quality" | True of the buffer-corrupted measurement, whose interval crossed zero. Corrected, V-only at c = 0.10 gives ΔNLL −0.0323, CI [−0.0382, −0.0258], with **16 of 16 documents improving** and the effect growing monotonically with the bound in aggregate. Reported as observed and unexplained; the claim the byte argument needs is only that the V budget costs nothing | `d_quality_findings.md` |
| 20 | "Pipelining was the one mechanism that could have flipped the decision, and it cannot, at any efficiency" | Generalises a two-stage overlap measurement to a seven-stage pipeline. A pipeline is bound by its busiest resource, which here is GPU0 at **21.43 ms** against a raw path of 23.64 ms — it wins, though not against a raw path also allowed to pipeline (11.82 ms). The break-even moves from 3.82 to **10.96 GB/s**. Implied by measured stage costs; the pipeline itself is still unmeasured | `gate2_findings.md` |
| 21 | *(withdrawn within the hour it was written)* The raw baseline is 18% too cheap because it reuses one buffer, so every ratio against it is inflated | **Wrong, and measured wrong.** Distinct buffers are 8% *faster* (25.94 ms against 28.18), so the reuse buys nothing. The 18% was the machine: `raw_iter` re-run on the pipeline pod takes 28.18 ms against the 23.64 recorded on the pod it came from, and the rebuilt loop takes 27.87 — agreeing to 1.1% on one host. No ratio changes. The reuse is still a correctness defect: 55 of 56 tensors arrive nowhere | `DEFECTS.md` D3 |
| 22 | On a durable MooseFS write compression should win **3×**, per the bandwidth sweep | Projected by dividing the byte reduction into an unchanged bandwidth. Measured, the offload write wins **1.21×–2.10×** across eight configurations and never 3.02×, because a 77.7 MB write is given **0.55×** the bandwidth a 234.9 MB write gets on that mount — the denominator of `bytes ÷ bandwidth` depends on its numerator. Same shape of error as assuming a codec's throughput is independent of tensor size | `pipeline_findings.md`, measured by `fsync_offload_findings.md` |
| 23 | The codec's fp32 error satisfies `E32 <= eps + 1e-6` | It does not, and no absolute tolerance is the right shape. cuSZp exceeds its declared bound by up to **1.0000015 x eps** -- a few float32 ulps *of eps*, which is what a quantiser working from a float32 `1/eps` does. The excess is relative, so it passes an absolute 1e-6 at small eps and fails at large: 0 of 448 in B0 (eps <= 2.2), 2 of 3584 in B1. The pre-declared tolerance was **not** widened; the margin is reported and a relative criterion is proposed for the next round | `protocol_2026_09_17/b0_findings.md` |
| 24 | A receiver can bound a copy or validate a payload using cuSZp's `cmpSize` | It cannot. `cuSZp_decompress_1D_fixed_f32` never reads the argument: declaring **64 bytes** in place of the true 628,536 returns a bit-identical correct reconstruction, and only removing the bytes breaks it (245 x eps). The decoder works from `nbEle` and consumes the buffer until done. This gives the previously-inferred zeroed-buffer contract a mechanism | `protocol_2026_09_17/b0_findings.md` |
| 25 | The K/V quality asymmetry, measured on 16 articles also used to develop it | Not withdrawn — **replicated**. On 32 held-out articles with no overlap: K-only +0.0155 NLL (26/32 worse), V-only −0.0278 (31/32 better), K+V now significant where the development interval crossed zero. Recorded here because the original was quotable only with its provenance attached, and now is not | `protocol_2026_09_17/b1_findings.md` |
| 26 | B0's reuse gate passed the protocol's reuse criterion | It passed a **weaker** one. The first run compared only the delivered bf16, on the reasoning that a bit-identical reconstruction has the same error by construction — true of the bf16 value, and silent about the fp32 one, since two fp32 reconstructions differing below half a bf16 ulp round to the same bf16. Likewise the two-device check judged a whole cache against its largest ε, under which a small-ε tensor can be wrong and pass. Both re-run and **both still pass** — 2240 rounds with 0 fp32 and 0 bf16 mismatches, and 1792 per-tensor byte-exact comparisons at depth 1 and 8 — but the original write-up claimed the stronger check while holding evidence for the weaker | `protocol_2026_09_17/b0_findings.md` |
| 27 | The full-forward / cached-forward gap "sits identically in every arm and cancels in every ΔNLL" | Asserted, not measured. The arms feed different values through the same kernels, so the bf16 noise is the same kind in each without being the same size, and nothing bounds the residual. What holds is narrower and sufficient: every arm is scored through the cached path, so no reported ΔNLL is a cached-against-full comparison. The gap is ~4e-03 against a smallest reported effect of 1.34e-02 | `protocol_2026_09_17/alignment_findings.md` |

Correction 3 is the one that reaches furthest: it invalidates aggregates in four
documents at once. Correction 5 is the one that was caught before it reached
anything published. Correction 9 is the one that cost the most to find — a single
undocumented API contract, and behind it three findings, two of which had already
been written up as results.

The register is not a list of carelessness. Corrections 1, 7 and the SZ3 revision
each replaced a claim with a *narrower and more testable* one, and the project is
better for each. What the register is for is that a reader can tell which of the
claims below are the survivors.
